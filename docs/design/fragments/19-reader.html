The Atlas Rollout Plan

MW Marcus Webb · 6 min read · 3 pages · 1,412 words

We're changing how Atlas ships. The June plan assumed a single launch day — press, pricing and product landing together on September 2. After three weeks of dogfooding and one humbling incident review, I no longer believe a big-bang launch is the responsible move. This message replaces the June plan; it freezes after Thursday's review.

Why we're changing the rollout

Two things changed. The checkout migration is running a week behind, and pretending otherwise only moves the slip somewhere more expensive. And the August 1 incident review showed our rollback path under load was theory, not practice. A staged rollout gives the fix three chances to fail safely before anyone important is watching.

  • Phases replace the launch day — each with an owner, an entry gate, and a rehearsed rollback.
  • Pricing ships dark in Phase 1. We meter real usage for two weeks before a customer sees a number.
  • No phase starts on a Friday. This is now written down.

"We'd rather slip a week we chose than lose a quarter we didn't."

One number decides everything: if weekly active usage in the partner cohort drops below 60%, we pause the GA date — no meeting, no debate. The decision is pre-made, which is the entire point of writing it down now, while nobody is tired and nothing is on fire.
